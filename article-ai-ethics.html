What Is the Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI?

Dr. Cindy Cushman
HR Expert & Consultant

To answer this question, we need to understand what constitutes an ethical dilemma. An ethical dilemma is a complex situation in which a person must choose among two or more conflicting moral principles, with no clear right or wrong option. The key feature of an ethical dilemma is that any decision made will violate some ethical norm, creating moral tension and uncertainty. Ethical dilemmas (also called moral dilemmas) occur when an individual, SMB, or group faces conflicting moral obligations or values and must decide which to prioritize. Unlike straightforward right-versus-wrong choices, ethical dilemmas involve conflicts between two right options.

In small businesses, owners often face conflicting duties or values when making AI-related decisions, constituting an ethical dilemma. Multiple ethical principles may apply, but following one can violate another. For example, in a small business, customer service is crucial. Many small businesses are known for their customer service models. To cut costs and stay competitive, an SMB owner might decide to add AI to its customer service decision process. This decision could eliminate jobs or make clients and customers feel they are not receiving the customer service they need or want as the human element has been removed. The upside includes cost savings and a competitive edge, while the downside is that the SMB could lose personal relationships with clients and customers, and it may impact employment. This presents an ethical dilemma for many SMB owners.

In certain situations, there may not be a clear solution, as every available option has negative moral consequences. A negative moral consequence refers to the harmful outcome that results from actions that violate moral or ethical principles. In simpler terms, it is the emotional, social, or spiritual fallout that occurs when someone engages in behavior deemed wrong or unethical.

For instance, consider the issue of privacy loss: surveillance tools and data-mining algorithms can gather and analyze personal information in ways that infringe upon privacy or consent. SMB owners must be aware of AI-driven surveillance tools that could potentially breach their customer agreements or employ data-mining technologies without user consent. Using AI in customer service decision-making may invade the privacy of an SMB’s clients and customers if the data-mining tool is unknown to them. When such infringements are discovered, the SMB owner may experience negative moral consequences because the AI services used compromised their customers’ privacy without consent.

Many SMB owners see AI as having a positive impact on their businesses, including increased efficiency, better customer insights to enhance marketing strategies, and improved decision-making through more detailed information and understanding. Conversely, an SMB owner can face significant consequences if they implement the wrong AI in their workplace. SMB business owners must weigh the benefits, risks, and ethical consequences of their AI-related decisions. This process is similar to the yin-yang concept, in which complementary forces work together to create balance and harmony. Although the stakes are high when it comes to integrating AI, each decision involves ethical considerations. SMB owners’ choices can impact people, relationships, or their company’s integrity.

How to Solve the Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI?

To address the Small Business Ethical Dilemma related to AI, it is essential to determine whether an ethical dilemma truly exists. SMB owners should ask themselves if the situation involves a moral or ethical conflict. What moral principles are in conflict? In essence, an SMB owner’s dilemma arises when every available choice violates some moral value or leads to harm that the owner did not anticipate. Therefore, reasoning and judgment—rather than merely following rules—are necessary for making the decision. When evaluating which course of action to take, SMB owners should consider both the short-term and long-term outcomes of each option, including emotional, social, and reputational effects. The goal is to identify the option that causes the least harm or generates the most good.

The SMB owner needs to assess their needs and decide if AI can effectively solve their challenges. It is crucial to have a clear understanding of the specific issues they face and to consider whether AI is genuinely the best solution or if they are just trying to jump on the AI bandwagon. Embedded in this decision-making process are the ethical considerations that have come up. The risks and ethical implications of using AI should be well understood during this evaluation. Additionally, it is important to think about whether using AI will not only address the current challenge but also improve the company’s efficiency, accuracy, and speed in the future. Forecasting whether AI will ultimately help or hurt the company in multiple ways, including the business owner’s moral compass, is essential in the decision-making process.

Ultimately, the SMB owner should choose the course of action they believe is most ethically justifiable and be prepared to explain their reasoning openly. There are three common frameworks for resolving ethical dilemmas that may help the SMB determine the best path forward and a way to communicate their decision.

1. Utilitarianism

This can best be described by the famous Spock quote from Star Trek:

“The needs of the many outweigh the needs of the one.”

This approach can be considered Utilitarianism—an ethical theory that judges an action’s morality based on its consequences, specifically how much overall happiness or well-being it produces. The central idea is to maximize utility, meaning to create the greatest good for the greatest number of people.

2. Deontology

This can best be described by the creator of deontological ethics, Immanuel Kant:

“Do the right thing because it is right.”

Deontological ethics emphasizes the importance of intentions and moral principles over the consequences of actions. Its primary focus is on duty and strict adherence to rules rather than the results of those actions.

3. Virtue Ethics

This can best be described by Aristotle’s philosophy:

“We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act but a habit.”

Virtue Ethics is an ethical approach primarily associated with Aristotle. It emphasizes a person’s character rather than adhering to specific rules (as in deontology) or focusing solely on outcomes (as in utilitarianism).

Conclusion

The Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI occurs when SMB owners must choose between conflicting moral values—such as cost efficiency vs. employee impact, or innovation vs. customer privacy—with no clear “right” answer. AI can improve efficiency, decision-making, and competitiveness, but it can also lead to job loss, reduced personal relationships, and privacy concerns.
